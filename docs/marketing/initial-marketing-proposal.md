# Riverly — Initial Marketing Proposal

**Status:** Working hypothesis for customer discovery and a roofing pilot  
**Date:** 15 October 2026  
**Audience:** Founders and early pilot partners

## Executive summary

Position Riverly initially as a focused lead-to-inspection workflow for roofing businesses—not as a general-purpose CRM, a replacement for a company's entire software stack, or a broad mobile-app launch.

The customer-facing problem to investigate is whether roofing teams lose time, context, or opportunities between receiving an inquiry and booking an inspection. Riverly's proposed role is to give the team a clear place to capture leads, assign ownership, record follow-up, and see what happened next.

This is a market hypothesis, not a validated promise. Before making performance claims or investing in paid acquisition, Riverly should run a hands-on pilot with a roofing company, document its existing process, and measure whether the product improves a meaningful outcome.

## Positioning hypothesis

### Primary audience

Roofing business owners and sales managers who:

- Receive enough inquiries that consistent follow-up is difficult to manage informally.
- Have multiple people handling sales, calls, or inspections.
- Use a mix of existing tools, spreadsheets, inboxes, or manual processes and do not want to replace their accounting or field-service systems just to improve lead handling.
- Can identify a person responsible for trying a new workflow and reviewing results.

These are qualification signals to test, not a confirmed ideal-customer profile. Company size, lead volume, current software, and buying authority should be learned during discovery rather than assumed from the industry label.

### Core problem

An incoming roofing inquiry can lose momentum when nobody is clearly responsible for the next step, follow-up is hard to see, or useful context is scattered across people and tools.

### Draft value proposition

> Riverly helps roofing teams keep leads, follow-up, and inspection progress in one clear workflow—alongside the tools they already use.

### Short positioning line

> Keep every roofing lead moving toward an inspection.

### Internal product promise

> Never lose track of a roofing lead or the work that follows.

Use the internal promise as a product north star, not as an unconditional public guarantee. It implies an outcome Riverly must demonstrate and could overstate what the current product can ensure.

## What Riverly is—and is not—selling

**Lead with:**

- A clear view of who owns each lead and what follow-up has happened.
- A shared history of calls, notes, and relevant job updates.
- A visible path from inquiry toward inspection and the next job milestone.
- A focused workflow that may complement a company's existing tools.

**Do not lead with:**

- “A configurable CRM platform” or a generic software feature list.
- “Replace ServiceTitan, Jobber, or your current systems.”
- AI, automated workflows, two-way accounting or payment integrations, or a contractor portal; these are not V1 commitments.
- “Social activity river,” awards, stickers, or profile features. Those are product concepts to validate, not established buyer benefits.
- Guaranteed conversion, revenue, response-time, or productivity improvements.

## Differentiation to test

Riverly's potential distinction is not the existence of pipeline stages or customization. It is whether a focused roofing workflow makes follow-up and handoffs more visible without forcing a business to migrate its whole operation.

The activity history may support this if users rely on it to answer practical questions quickly:

- Has anyone contacted this homeowner, and what happened?
- Who owns the next step?
- Is an inspection booked, and what information should the inspector have?
- What does the team know about this lead or job so far?

Test those tasks against the company's current method. Do not assume that a chronological feed is valuable simply because it is comprehensive.

## Pilot offer

Recruit one roofing business for a guided pilot focused on lead handling through inspection booking.

### Pilot scope

- Capture a defined set of new leads using the intake methods available in the pilot.
- Assign a responsible salesperson.
- Record outreach attempts and outcomes without treating a user-entered log as independently verified communication.
- Track follow-up and inspection-booking progress.
- Review a simple manager view of lead ownership and status.
- Keep the company's existing accounting and field-service systems in place.

Do not promise seamless integration with a system until the specific integration and data flow exist and have been tested. For tools Riverly does not connect to, agree with the pilot partner on a low-friction process and be transparent about any duplicate entry.

### Pilot success measures

Establish the baseline with the partner before the pilot, using agreed definitions and comparable lead sources. Track:

1. Time from lead receipt to first outreach, distinguishing verified communication from manually recorded activity.
2. Share of eligible leads with an assigned owner and a visible next step.
3. Share of eligible leads that book an inspection.
4. Time spent finding lead or follow-up context, measured through observation or a consistent user-reported method.
5. Data-entry burden, duplicate entry, and the share of leads that fail to make it into Riverly.

Set numeric success thresholds with the pilot company after its baseline and data quality are understood. Do not select a favorable metric after seeing the results. A pilot should also record seasonality, lead source mix, staffing changes, and other factors that could explain a change.

## Early acquisition approach

Use founder-led, relationship-based discovery before paid marketing:

1. Speak with roofing owners and sales managers about their current lead-to-inspection process.
2. Ask them to walk through recent real examples, including missed or delayed follow-up—not just describe their ideal process.
3. Invite a qualified business to co-design and run the pilot.
4. Use referrals from roofing operators, consultants, and relevant local business networks to find additional interviewees.
5. After pilot evidence exists, test targeted outreach and partner channels with a concise demonstration of the validated workflow.

Avoid broad paid campaigns, a large feature-led launch, or claims based on anecdotal feedback until Riverly knows which customer profile responds and what outcome it can credibly demonstrate.

## Initial marketing assets

Create only the materials needed to recruit and run the pilot:

- **One-page landing page:** audience, lead-follow-up problem, concise positioning, a product walkthrough, and a clear pilot-interest call to action.
- **Short demo:** show one representative lead from intake through assignment, outreach logging, and inspection status. Use realistic sample data.
- **Pilot overview:** explain the scope, partner responsibilities, data handling, measurement plan, and what is not included.
- **Discovery script:** ask about current behavior and recent examples before presenting Riverly.
- **Objection notes:** record concerns about duplicate entry, existing software, setup time, team adoption, and data migration.

Keep claims limited to capabilities that are implemented and available in the version being demonstrated. Label mockups and planned capabilities as such.

## Message examples

### Homepage headline

**Keep roofing leads moving toward an inspection.**

### Supporting copy

Riverly gives your team a shared view of incoming leads, ownership, follow-up, and inspection progress—without asking you to replace your entire business software stack.

### Call to action

**Help shape the roofing pilot**

### Outreach opener

We're exploring a focused way for roofing teams to keep lead ownership and follow-up visible through inspection booking. Could you walk us through how a recent inquiry moved from first contact to its next step—and where the process was difficult?

These are draft messages. “Without asking you to replace your entire business software stack” should only be used if the actual pilot does not require that replacement and any unavoidable manual work is clearly explained.

## Discovery questions

Use neutral questions and ask for recent examples:

- Where do new leads come from, and how are they captured today?
- What happens after a lead arrives? Who decides who follows up?
- How do you know whether and when someone contacted the homeowner?
- What is the most recent lead that was delayed, forgotten, or difficult to hand off? What caused it?
- How are inspections scheduled and their results shared with the sales team?
- Which systems are the source of truth for contact details, lead status, and job status?
- Where would Riverly create duplicate entry? Which information would need to sync, and how soon?
- What would need to improve for your team to keep using a new tool after a pilot?
- Who would approve, configure, and pay for a tool like this?

Avoid asking only whether someone “likes the idea.” Seek evidence of current pain, workarounds, decision authority, and willingness to participate in a real test.

## Risks and response

| Risk | Marketing response |
|---|---|
| Riverly sounds like another generic CRM | Describe the specific lead-to-inspection use case and demonstrate it with a realistic workflow. |
| Teams resist a second system | Identify the source of truth for each data type; disclose gaps and measure duplicate-entry burden in the pilot. |
| Activity history becomes extra data entry | Test whether the record helps users coordinate or find context; simplify or remove low-value logging. |
| Product claims outrun V1 capability | Separate available features from roadmap; make no outcome guarantees before measured evidence. |
| Roofing businesses vary substantially | Treat the target profile as a hypothesis and qualify by process, lead volume, team ownership, and current tools. |
| Pilot improvement is caused by other changes | Agree on baseline and definitions in advance; document lead mix, staffing, and process changes. |

## Decision gates

### Before recruiting a pilot

- Confirm the pilot can capture leads with a process the company will actually use.
- Agree on existing-system boundaries and where duplicate entry may occur.
- Select baseline measures and define “lead received,” “first outreach,” and “inspection booked.”
- Confirm who will use the product and who will evaluate the pilot.

### Before public launch or paid acquisition

- Complete at least one pilot with interpretable baseline and outcome data.
- Demonstrate that users adopt the workflow without unsustainable founder intervention.
- Confirm the product does not create unacceptable duplicate entry or data-quality gaps.
- Identify a repeatable customer profile and acquisition path.
- Use only substantiated claims in public messaging.

## Relationship to the product specification

This proposal narrows the customer-facing message and pilot objective; it does not change the product specification or ratified V1 requirements. The product documents describe broader capabilities, while this proposal recommends testing lead handling through inspection booking as the initial market wedge. Any reduction to V1 implementation scope should be recorded in the product requirements and acceptance criteria separately.

## Open questions

- Which lead sources must be supported for a first pilot to be useful?
- What is the minimum viable approach to avoid duplicate entry with a pilot company's current systems?
- Which user is the daily champion, and who is the economic buyer?
- What lead volume and team structure make the problem urgent enough to pay to solve?
- Does the activity history materially improve handoffs or just record them?
- What measured outcome would justify continued use and a paid contract?
